Master PM (CM)

Overview​

The Master PM (CM) application enables the creation of master maintenance records which are linked to one or more CM Items.

The application provides additional functionality, for configuration-managed assets, over and above that provided by standard Maximo.

Additional functionality provided in the Master PM (CM) application:​

CM Items​

The CM Items table provides the ability to associate one or more CM Items with a Master PM.
This enables the system to automatically create PMs from the Master PMs for configuration-managed assets with the CM Item listed on the Master PM.

An action "Create Associated PMs for CM Items" is also provided to facilitate the immediate creation of PMs from the Master PM (for the applicable configuration-managed assets).

PMs which are created automatically are marked as needing review to ensure the next date or meter count at which they will become due is accurate. A dedicated BDI Problem Code: ENR (Event Needs Review) is raised for PMs where this review needs to take place.

Status and Revisions​

ACM introduces a Status field to the Master PM record (MASTERPM.PLUSASTATUS). When a new Master PM is created it has an initial status of DRAFT. This enables a user to setup the Master PM and configure it's various fields eg frequency criteria before making the Master PM Active. PMs cannot be created from the Master PM unless it is Active. In addition the system will not automatically create PMs from the Master PM (for applicable Assets) until the Master PM is made Active.

ACM also introduces Revisioning functionality to the Master PM (CM) and PM (CM) applications. This operates in a similar way to revision functionality in other Maximo applications for example Job Plans.

Master PM/PM Revision functionality is disabled in ACM by default. It must be enabled within the Organizations (CM) application. This is a system level setting and is contained within the CM Options section of the System Settings dialog. The option is a checbox labelled "Revision Control for Master PM / PM?".

Once enabled, Master PM and PM records become read-only for configuration-managed assets. In order to make changes the record must be revised. This revision action creates a "copy" of the record with a pending revision (PNDREV) status. Once the necessary updates are made to this record it can be made active. The previous revision is placed into history with a status of REVISED. This enables full tracking of what has changed, when and by whom.

The Build Data Interpreter will cause the update of the associated PMs automatically when it next validates the affected assets. Alternatively the action "Update Associated PMs?" will also trigger this immediately.

Extendable PMs​

The Extendable checkbox, when set to true, indicates that the associated PMs as having the ability to be extended. A PM Extension is a controlled process to allow the Due date or Due Count to be extended for the next occurence of the PM. The PM Extension can be created in the PM (CM) application or the PM Extensions (CM) application. A workflow can be created against a PM Extension to create an approval process to activate the extension. PM Extensions are discussed in more detail in a separate topic.

Frequencies​

The Frequency tab on a Master PM defines when preventive maintenance work orders should be generated. Frequencies can be time-based, meter-based, or both. When both are configured, the PM becomes due when whichever threshold is reached first.

Alert and Warning Interval State​

The Alert and Warning Interval State radio button controls how the Alert Interval and Warning Interval values are interpreted. Three options are available:

OptionDescription
Absolute from ActiveThe alert and warning thresholds are measured forward from the date/reading at which the PM became active.
Percent of Frequency from ActiveThe alert and warning values are treated as a percentage of the total frequency, measured forward from the active date/reading.
Absolute prior to Due(Default) The alert and warning values represent an amount subtracted back from the due date or due count. For example, an Alert Interval of 2,000 km means the alert fires 2,000 km before the PM is due.

The default setting is Absolute prior to Due, which means the intervals count backwards from the due threshold rather than forwards from a start point.

Time Based Frequency​

Time-based frequencies trigger PM work orders based on elapsed calendar time.

Example: A quarterly engine inspection might be configured as:

  • Frequency: 90 days
  • Alert Interval: 14 days (alert raised 14 days before the PM is due)
  • Warning Interval: 7 days (warning raised 7 days before the PM is due)

With the default Absolute prior to Due state:

  • The alert fires 14 calendar days before the due date.
  • The warning fires 7 calendar days before the due date.
  • The PM becomes due on the calculated due date.

Meter Based Frequency​

Meter-based frequencies trigger PM work orders based on a meter reading reaching a threshold value. The meter type is specified by selecting a meter on the frequency row (for example, ODOM-KM — Odometer Reading in Kilometers).

Example: A bogie frame and suspension inspection might be configured as:

  • Meter: ODOM-KM (Odometer Reading in Kilometers)
  • Frequency: 500,000.00 km
  • Alert Interval: 2,000.00 km (alert raised 2,000 km before the PM is due)
  • Warning Interval: 1,000.00 km (warning raised 1,000 km before the PM is due)
  • First Start Reading: 0.00 km

With the default Absolute prior to Due state:

  • The alert fires when the odometer reading reaches 498,000 km (500,000 − 2,000).
  • The warning fires when the odometer reading reaches 499,000 km (500,000 − 1,000).
  • The PM becomes due at 500,000 km.

BDI Problem Codes Generated by Frequency Thresholds​

As the BDI (Build Data Interpreter) evaluates each asset against its PMs, it raises problem codes based on how the current date or meter reading compares with the frequency thresholds defined above:

Problem CodeNameDescription
EBAEvent Beyond AlertThe current date or meter reading has passed the Alert threshold. A maintenance event is approaching and should be planned.
EBWEvent Beyond WarningThe current date or meter reading has passed the Warning threshold. The maintenance event is imminent and scheduling should be confirmed.
EBDEvent Beyond DueThe current date or meter reading has passed the Due threshold. The maintenance event is overdue and a work order should be raised immediately.

These codes are raised in ascending order of urgency: EBA → EBW → EBD. Once a work order is generated and the PM is completed, the next due date/count is recalculated and the cycle restarts.

Soft Tasks​

The Soft Task checkbox on the Frequency tab changes the set of BDI problem codes raised for the PM.

When a Master PM is marked as a soft task, the BDI raises the following problem codes in place of the standard EBA / EBW / EBD codes:

Problem CodeNameDescription
SBASoft Task Beyond AlertThe current date or meter reading has passed the Alert threshold for a soft task PM.
SBWSoft Task Beyond WarningThe current date or meter reading has passed the Warning threshold for a soft task PM.
SBDSoft Task Beyond DueThe current date or meter reading has passed the Due threshold for a soft task PM.

The key distinction between a soft task and a standard PM is that SBD is not considered an invalid configuration. Overdue soft tasks do not represent a safety-critical or compliance failure — they are desirable but not mandatory maintenance activities.

A typical example of a soft task is vehicle cleaning. Missing a scheduled wash does not affect the safe operation of the asset, so the BDI treats the overdue condition as an alert rather than as a compliance violation. The default severity of the problem codes can be overriden at indivdual Model level for exmaple if INFO is preferred to ALERT for a Soft Task.

Frequency Iterations​

Frequency Iterations allow the frequency — and optionally the associated Job Plan — of a PM to change in a predefined sequence as the PM is completed over time. This is useful where the scope or interval of maintenance varies at different points in an asset's life cycle.

Frequency Iterations cannot be used in combination with standard Maximo Job Plan Sequencing. They serve as an alternative mechanism for varying the Job Plan across PM occurrences.

Iteration Definition​

Each iteration row in the frequency table contains the following key fields:

FieldDescription
Iteration Sequence NumberDetermines the order in which iterations are activated. Lower numbers are activated first.
FrequencyThe time or meter interval that applies while this iteration is active.
Alert Interval / Warning IntervalThe alert and warning thresholds that apply during this iteration.
Job PlanThe Job Plan to be used when generating work orders during this iteration.
Iterations of this FrequencyThe number of times this frequency must be completed before the next iteration is activated. If left empty on the last iteration, the PM will continue indefinitely at that iteration's frequency, regardless of the Loop Iterations setting.

Iteration Tracking on the PM​

For each PM created from the Master PM, the system maintains:

  • An Iteration Counter — tracks how many completions have occurred at the current iteration.
  • A Frequency Active flag on each iteration row — indicates which iteration is currently active.

When the required number of completions for the current iteration is reached, the system automatically activates the next iteration in the sequence, updating the frequency and Job Plan accordingly.

When the Iteration Counter is Incremented​

The point at which the iteration counter is incremented depends on the Use Last Work Order's Start Information to Calculate Next Due Frequency checkbox on the Frequency tab:

PM TypeCheckbox SettingIteration Counter Incremented
Fixed PMChecked (Yes)When a work order is generated from the PM.
Floating PMUnchecked (No)When the work order is completed.

Loop Iterations​

The Loop Iterations checkbox controls what happens when the last iteration in the sequence is reached:

  • Checked — the sequence loops back to the first iteration and the cycle repeats.
  • Unchecked — once the last iteration is completed the PM is made inactive.

If the last iteration has the Iterations of this Frequency field left empty, the PM will continue indefinitely at the last iteration's frequency and the Loop Iterations checkbox has no effect.

Example Iteration Definitions​

Example 1 — Escalating inspection scope after overhaul (meter based)

A bogie assembly requires a light inspection every 100,000 km for the first occurrence after overhaul, then transitions to a full inspection every 50,000 km for all subsequent occurrences. Loop Iterations is checked so the full inspection repeats indefinitely.

SeqFrequencyJob PlanIterations of this Frequency
1100,000 kmRTMT-PI-JP1 (Light Inspection)1
250,000 kmRTMT-PI-JP2 (Full Inspection)(empty — runs indefinitely)

Example 2 — New asset burn-in then standard cadence (time based)

A newly commissioned asset requires monthly checks for the first three months, then moves to a standard quarterly inspection. Loop Iterations is unchecked so the quarterly inspection continues indefinitely via the empty Iterations field on the last row.

SeqFrequencyJob PlanIterations of this Frequency
130 daysJP-MONTHLY-CHECK3
290 daysJP-QUARTERLY-CHECK(empty — runs indefinitely)

Example 3 — Cyclic major/minor service pattern (meter based)

A traction motor follows a repeating pattern of three minor services followed by one major service, then the cycle repeats. Loop Iterations is checked.

SeqFrequencyJob PlanIterations of this Frequency
150,000 kmJP-MINOR-SERVICE3
250,000 kmJP-MAJOR-SERVICE1

With Loop Iterations checked, the sequence restarts after the major service, producing the pattern: minor → minor → minor → major → minor → minor → minor → major → …

Master PM / PM Actions​

The Master PM Actions provides the ability to define actions for the current "primary" PM which cause an action to be performed against a "secondary" Master PM/PM. These actions are automatically executed when specific events occur with the system such as work order completion or asset removal and install.

The Master/PM actions are limited to primary / secondary PMs for the same CM Item or configuration-managed asset. That is to say, it is not possible to define an action on a primary PM, for a configuration-managed asset, which effects a secondary PM for a different configuration-managed asset.

A list of the available action types, their associated effect and what triggers the action is detailed below:

Action TypeTriggered WhenEffect of Action
ACTIVATEA work order generated from the primary PM is completed.The status of the secondary PM is changed to ACTIVE.
COMPLETEA work order generated from the primary PM is completed.- The open maintenance event for the Secondary PM is marked as claimed and moved into history.
- The next due date/count of the secondary PM is also reset based on the actual finish date of the primary PM work order.
- If an open work order exists for the secondary PM its status is changed to COMPLETE
CREATE WOA work order generated from the primary PM is completed.A work order is generated from the Secondary PM
DEACTIVATEA work order generated from the primary PM is completed.The status of the secondary PM is changed to INACTIVE.
OFF-ACTIVATEThe asset associated with the primary PM is removed from a parent assembly.The status of the secondary PM is changed to ACTIVE.
OFF-DEACTIVATEThe asset associated with the primary PM is removed from a parent assembly.The status of the secondary PM is changed to INACTIVE.
ON-ACTIVATEThe asset associated with the primary PM is installed into a parent assembly.The status of the secondary PM is changed to ACTIVE.
ON-DEACTIVATEThe asset associated with the primary PM is installed into a parent assembly.The status of the secondary PM is changed to INACTIVE.
